# Methodology

## 1. Model Design

This project models a synthetic microbial chassis designed to survive under conditions that would challenge conventional terrestrial organisms. The model is intentionally qualitative and conceptual, but it is structured around explicit biological assumptions. It treats the organism as a living system with defined states of activity, dormancy, and adaptation.

The model is built around three key assumptions:

1. The organism possesses a stress-tolerant cellular architecture capable of surviving in extreme environments.
2. Reproductive viability depends on solvent activity, mechanical stability, and intracellular chemical continuity.
3. Long-term environmental persistence produces evolutionary specialization through selection and mutation.

## 2. Environmental Parameters

The simulation tracks several environmental variables, each of which influences the organism's physiology and survival strategy. These variables include:

- temperature
- pressure
- water activity
- solvent availability
- radiation exposure
- atmospheric density
- nutrient accessibility

These variables are used to define the organism's active state, dormant state, and adaptive trajectory across distinct planetary environments.

## 3. Active State Model

The active state occurs when liquid solvent is available and the organism can maintain metabolic function. In this state, the cell remains capable of reproduction by binary fission. However, division is not assumed to be mechanically effortless. The model explicitly incorporates the challenge of maintaining division machinery under extreme heat and high pressure.

Conventional cell division depends on cytoskeletal organization and spatially regulated septation. To preserve division under severe environmental stress, the model assumes the presence of pressure-stable and thermally resilient division proteins, along with chaperone-like structural support. These features allow the cell to continue reproduction when environmental conditions are harsh but not fatal.

## 4. Dormant State Model

The dormant state begins when water activity falls below the threshold required for active metabolism. In this condition, the organism cannot divide or maintain normal growth. The simulation assumes that a tardigrade-inspired mechanism triggers vitrification-like arrest. In this state, intracellular chemistry becomes glass-like and effectively nonreactive, preventing DNA replication and other metabolic functions.

This model treats dormancy as a reversible survival state rather than a form of death. The organism remains viable while metabolically inactive, preserving its structural integrity until an environment once again supports active biological function.

## 5. Evolutionary Model

The long-term evolutionary component of the model assumes that thousands of generations under a single environmental regime will produce a stable selective signature. This process is modeled as a combination of directed evolution and mutational drift. Traits that increase survival in the prevailing environment become fixed, while costly or unnecessary functions are reduced, silenced, or deleted.

This is represented in the simulation by predicted ecological divergence between distinct planetary strains. Each lineage develops traits optimized for the dominant selection pressure of its environment rather than for universal resilience.

## 6. Data Interpretation and Limitations

This methodology is conceptual and explanatory rather than experimentally validated. It provides a formal structure for understanding how a synthetic organism might behave under extreme environments, but it does not claim to represent a verified biological system. Its principal value lies in its ability to integrate environmental constraints, reproductive behavior, and evolutionary adaptation into a coherent hypothesis.

The model therefore functions as a framework for future study. It can be expanded with quantitative thresholds, computational simulation, or experimental validation, but its current form serves as a structured speculative model for biological persistence in extreme planetary conditions.
