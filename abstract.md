# Abstract

This thesis examines the design and evolutionary behavior of a synthetic extremophile microbial chassis intended to persist in hostile planetary environments. The model considers three environmental regimes: the high-temperature atmosphere of Venus, the high-pressure subsurface environment of Europa, and the vacuum-dominated conditions of deep space. The organism is modeled as a biologically robust synthetic chassis capable of active replication when sufficient liquid solvent is present, dormancy when water activity becomes critically low, and long-term adaptive specialization under sustained environmental selection pressures.

The research focuses on the relationship between reproduction, environmental stress, and evolutionary change. In the active state, the organism maintains growth and division under challenging conditions through stress-resistant division machinery and enhanced structural stability. In the dormant state, the organism transitions to a vitrification-like condition in which metabolism and replication are suspended, allowing long-term survival under vacuum, desiccation, and radiation exposure. Over thousands of generations, the population undergoes directed evolution and mutational drift, producing form-specific adaptations organized around local environmental conditions.

This project integrates concepts from synthetic biology, extremophile physiology, and astrobiology to propose a conceptual model for how life-like systems could persist in extraterrrestrial environments. The findings suggest that environmental pressure is not simply a source of mortality, but also a driver of specialized adaptation. In this view, life in extreme environments may be characterized by reversible survival states and niche-specific evolutionary strategies rather than a single universal physiology.

The thesis argues that synthetic extremophile systems provide a valuable framework for understanding biological persistence in hostile environments and for informing future models of extraterrestrial habitability.
