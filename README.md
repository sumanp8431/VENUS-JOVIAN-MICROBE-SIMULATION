# Synthetic Extremophile Biology for Planetary Habitability Modeling

This repository presents a thesis-style framework for modeling a synthetic microbial organism designed to survive in extreme planetary environments, including Venusian atmospheric conditions, Europan high-pressure regimes, and vacuum-dominated deep-space conditions.

## Repository Structure

- `THESIS_OUTLINE.md` — formal thesis structure and chapter roadmap
- `abstract.md` — concise summary of the research problem, methods, and conclusions
- `introduction.md` — background, motivation, research questions, and hypotheses
- `methodology.md` — model design, assumptions, and environmental parameters
- `results_and_analysis.md` — active state, dormant state, and evolutionary outcomes
- `discussion.md` — significance, limitations, and implications for astrobiology
- `conclusion.md` — final synthesis and future work
- `references.md` — academic bibliography and source framework
- `reproductive_longterm_simulation.md` — detailed model of reproduction and adaptive evolution

## Research Focus

The project examines how a synthetic microbial host could maintain viability under extreme stress by integrating:

- active reproduction under liquid-solvent conditions,
- reversible dormancy under vacuum and desiccation,
- long-term evolutionary specialization in response to environmental selection pressures.

## Overall Thesis Argument

Environmental conditions determine the life-history trajectory of the organism. Under favorable solvent conditions, the microbe remains active and reproduces. Under desiccation and vacuum, it enters a vitrified dormant state to preserve viability. Over evolutionary timescales, repeated environmental pressure leads to niche-specific adaptation and biological specialization.

This repository is organized as a thesis-ready framework for future expansion into a full academic document.
